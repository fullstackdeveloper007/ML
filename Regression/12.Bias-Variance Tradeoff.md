# Bias-Variance Tradeoff in Machine Learning

In machine learning, understanding the **bias-variance tradeoff** is crucial for building models that generalize well to unseen data.

---

## 🎯 What Is Bias?

Bias refers to the **error due to overly simplistic assumptions** in the learning algorithm.

* High bias means the model **underfits** the data.
* Common in models like **linear regression** when the data is non-linear.

### 🔎 Example:

A straight line trying to fit a curved dataset. The model misses the patterns — this is underfitting.

---

## 🔀 What Is Variance?

Variance refers to the **model's sensitivity to small changes** in the training data.

* High variance means the model **overfits** the data.
* Common in **complex models** like deep trees or high-degree polynomials.

### 🔎 Example:

A highly wiggly curve fitting every point of the training data, but failing on new data — this is overfitting.

---

## ⚖️ The Tradeoff

* **High Bias + Low Variance**: Simple model, misses patterns, poor training and test performance.
* **Low Bias + High Variance**: Complex model, great training performance but poor test generalization.
* **Goal**: Find a **sweet spot** between bias and variance for best test accuracy.

### Visualization (Intuition)

```
Generalization Error = Bias^2 + Variance + Irreducible Error
```

* **Bias^2**: Error from erroneous assumptions.
* **Variance**: Error from model fluctuations.
* **Irreducible Error**: Noise inherent in data.

---

## 📌 How to Handle the Tradeoff

* **Increase Bias (reduce variance)**

  * Simpler model
  * Fewer features
  * More regularization (L1/L2)

* **Decrease Bias (increase variance)**

  * More complex model
  * More training data
  * Decrease regularization

---

## 🧠 Why It Matters

Balancing bias and variance helps:

* Build robust models
* Avoid underfitting and overfitting
* Achieve better **generalization** on new data

---
